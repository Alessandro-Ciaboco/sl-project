The problem & the goal

Business problem

A retail company sends the same offer to all customers without any targeting. This wastes budget on people who won’t convert and misses those most likely to buy.

Two-part approach

Part 1: PCA + K-Means to find natural customer segments.

Part 2: Four classification models to predict who will accept the next campaign.

2,240 customers
29 variables
~15% positive response rate
5 past campaigns
Cross-sectional dataset. One row per customer, iid. Mixed types: numeric, categorical, binary.

Dataset: Variable groups & features

Demographics & Household

Core customer profiling attributes used to establish baseline socioeconomic status.

Age Income Education Marital_Status Kidhome Teenhome

Product Spending Profile

Two-year historical spend data across six primary retail categories. Aggregated into a master TotalSpent feature to represent absolute buying power.

Wines Fruits Meat Fish Sweets Gold

Channel Engagement & Traffic

Interaction metrics detailing exactly how the customer shops. Separating visits from purchases is critical to identify browsers.

  • NumWebVisitsMonth: Digital traffic proxy
  • NumWebPurchases: E-commerce conversion
  • NumStorePurchases: Physical retail
  • NumCatalogPurchases: Direct mail

Campaign History & The Target

Historical record of promotional acceptance across 5 previous marketing initiatives.

Target: ResponseTarget (Yes/No). Encodes acceptance of campaign 6 and is the label for all supervised models.

Feature Engineering: Raw dates (Year_Birth) were transformed into Age for compatibility with distance-based algorithms.

Data Cleaning: Ensuring Result Integrity

1 Handling Missing Values

Rows without income data were removed to prevent estimation bias. Imputation was avoided as it could introduce artificial signals in financial modeling.

Removed: Income (NA)

2 Outlier Suppression

Strict filtering to stabilize variance. We capped Age at 100 and Income at 600k to remove obvious data-entry errors.

Filtered: Age > 100 Filtered: Income > 600k

3 Zero-Variance Features

Variables with constant values across all observations provide zero predictive power. Their removal reduces computational noise.

Dropped: Z_CostContact Dropped: Z_Revenue

4 Administrative Noise

Row identifiers and join dates were excluded to prevent models from learning coincidental patterns.

Dropped: ID Dropped: Dt_Customer

Rationale: The cleaning phase focused on improving the signal-to-noise ratio. By prioritising data quality over quantity, we ensure that the subsequent PCA and predictive models are grounded in realistic behavioural patterns.

Clustering: Choosing optimal k

The Elbow Method

Evaluating the Within-Cluster Sum of Squares reveals a clear bend at k = 3.

Why stop at 3?

Increasing the number of clusters beyond 3 flattens the curve, meaning we would add complexity without capturing fundamentally different consumer behaviours.

Cluster Deep Dive: Marketing Implications

Behind the Premium numbers

Spending is wine-heavy and catalog-driven. They buy less often but order big. The recency is low, showing they respond to seasonal events.

Middle: the treat-them-right tier

Balanced basket across categories. They use both web and store. Moderate recency: regular but not impulsive.

Browsers are more than clicks

They visit the site a lot but almost never buy. Their income is not zero, so price is not the only barrier. They might need trust or a simpler first step.

From segments to strategy

Premium: early access, VIP events.
Middle: bundle deals, cross-sell.
Browsers: free shipping on first order, low-risk trial.

Key insight: Web visits are negatively correlated with spending. So heavy site traffic is not a sales signal; it often flags people who are still comparing or window‑shopping.

Part 2: Supervised Learning

Setup

Target Variable: ResponseTarget (binary Yes/No), indicating campaign 6 acceptance.

Class imbalance: ~15% positive responses. AUC‑ROC was strictly chosen as the evaluation metric to prevent misleading accuracy scores.

5-fold cross-validation

Performed only on the training set (80% of data). Each fold is used once as validation. Prevents overfitting and provides stable AUC estimates before touching the holdout set.

80% training (with CV)
20% final test

Models evaluated

Logistic regression

Lasso

Random Forest

Artificial Neural Network (ANN)

Stratified split keeps the ~15% Yes rate equal in train and test. Random Forest and ANN were tuned with CV; Logistic/Lasso used CV+upsampling.

Random forest result: Drivers of Conversion

Recency is #1

Days since last purchase dominates. Recent buyers are drastically more likely to convert.

Spending & Economic power

Economic power rank just below Recency, represented by Income and TotalSpent.

Demographics Lag Behind

Age sits at the bottom. The model explicitly confirms that behaviour predicts conversion better than profile.

Model Comparison: 5-Fold CV AUC-ROC

Random Forest Wins

Highest median AUC and the tightest interquartile range across folds, proving to be the most accurate and consistent model.

ANN is Unstable

Shows much higher fold-to-fold variance and the lowest mean, making it less reliable in production.

Linear Models Underperform

Logistic and Lasso are good but have bigger variance. The performance gap confirms the decision boundary is non‑linear.

Business Logic: Sending a digital offer costs essentially zero. Accepting more false positives to catch real converters maximises ROI.

Key takeaways

01: Ditch the blanket approach

Clustering reveals three economically distinct tiers. Segment‑specific strategies are the logical baseline, targeting Premium buyers and Browsers with different incentives.

02: Behaviour predicts conversion better than demographics

Recency and past campaign acceptance are far stronger predictors than age or marital status. Marketing efforts must prioritise recent engagement signals.

03: Threshold tuning guarantees ROI

Deployed at an adjusted threshold of 0.3, the Random Forest model becomes a cost‑effective screening tool that recovers a large share of potential revenue with minimal overhead.
